Every Vanderbilt job runs through HCM.

Welcome to HCM Academy.

HCM stands for Human Capital Management. It is the system, and the everyday work around it, that Vanderbilt uses to hire people, pay them, track their time and leave, and keep every job and funding source accurate. At Vanderbilt that work happens in Oracle HCM Cloud. If you post a job, approve a timecard, set up a position, or move someone to a new department, you are doing HCM work, and the record you leave behind decides whether a person is paid correctly and charged to the right account.

HCM Academy is where you learn that work, one task at a time. By the end of the series, you will be able to:

  • Explain how a position, an assignment, and a person record fit together, and why the order you enter them matters
  • Complete the common transactions in your role, from a new requisition to an ended assignment, correctly the first time
  • Connect each change you make to what it touches downstream: pay, benefits, leave, and funding
  • Find the right course, guide, or contact for the task in front of you

You are here · Before the courses What HCM is · The big picture

The role of HCM.

Think of HCM as Vanderbilt’s single record of who works here, what job they hold, what they are paid, and who pays for it. Payroll, benefits, leave, and financial reporting all read from that one record, so a change you make in one place shows up everywhere else.

The idea

Most HCM problems are timing and order problems. A position has to exist before you can hire into it. An assignment change needs the right effective date before payroll runs. Costing has to change when the funding changes. Learn the order, and most of the work gets simpler.

What HCM does

Four jobs the system does for you.

Keeps the record

One person record holds each employee’s job, position, department, manager, pay, and work history.

Pays people

Timecards, salary, and one-time payments flow from the record into each payroll run.

Charges the right account

Costing sends each salary dollar to the department, fund, or grant that should pay it.

Protects people

Leave, eligibility, and approvals keep Vanderbilt in step with policy and the law.

Learning area 01 of 06 Area 01 · Recruitment & Hiring

From open position to accepted offer.

Recruiting covers the request to fill a position, the job posting, reviewing applicants, interviews, and the offer a candidate accepts. Details entered here travel with the new hire into pay, benefits, and funding.

The idea

Build the position first, then the requisition, then the offer. Each step copies details from the one before it, so a mistake early on shows up in every step after.

What you will practice
  • Open a requisition against the right position
  • Move candidates through each hiring stage
  • Extend an offer and hand the new hire off to onboarding

Learning area 02 of 06 Area 02 · Payroll & Timecards

Getting people paid right, on time.

Hourly staff record their time, managers approve it, and payroll processes it on a fixed schedule. Salaried pay, rate changes, and one-time payments flow from the same record.

The idea

Payroll pays what the record says. Most pay problems trace back to a timecard that was late, unapproved, or coded wrong, or to a job or pay record that was out of date when payroll ran.

What you will practice
  • Review, correct, and approve timecards before the deadline
  • Read the payroll calendar and know your cutoffs
  • Process pay rate changes and one-time payments

Learning area 03 of 06 Area 03 · Onboarding, Offboarding, Transfers & Managing Positions

Every job lives in a position.

Positions are the budgeted seats in a department. Assignments connect a person to a position. This area covers the life of both: setting up a seat, bringing someone into it, moving them, and closing things out when they leave.

The idea

Picture the position as the chair and the assignment as the person sitting in it. You can change the chair, move the person to a different chair, or have them stand up and leave. Each of those is a different transaction with its own effective date.

What you will practice
  • Prepare a new hire’s record and first-day tasks
  • Create a position or update its details
  • Process transfers, manager changes, and other assignment changes
  • Process terminations and end assignments cleanly

Learning area 04 of 06 Area 04 · Leave Management

Time away, tracked with care.

Leave covers everyday absences like vacation and sick time, and protected leaves of absence such as FMLA. It also covers the return to work, when the record has to be closed out.

The idea

Leave touches pay, benefits, and legal protections at the same time. Enter it promptly, choose the right absence type, and close it out when the employee comes back.

What you will practice
  • Record and approve absences with the right type
  • Read balances and accruals
  • Start, extend, and close a leave of absence

Learning area 05 of 06 Area 05 · Costing Management

Follow each salary dollar to its account.

Costing tells the system which departments, funds, or grants pay for a person’s salary, and in what share. It matters most when funding changes: a new grant, a grant ending, or a split between departments.

The idea

Costing answers one question: who pays for this work? When the answer changes, the costing has to change too, with the right effective date. Otherwise salary lands on the wrong account and has to be moved later.

What you will practice
  • Read a costing setup and its percentage splits
  • Update costing when funding starts, ends, or changes
  • Spot costing that will expire before it causes a problem

Learning area 06 of 06 Area 06 · Student Hiring and Assignment Changes

Student jobs, from start to finish.

Student employment has its own rules: eligibility checks, limits on hours during the term, Federal Work-Study funding, and jobs that end with the semester. This area covers hiring students and changing their assignments as the year moves on.

The idea

Student jobs follow the academic calendar. Check eligibility before the start date, give every assignment an end date, and update the record when the semester or the funding changes.

What you will practice
  • Hire a student into the right position and job type
  • Set up Work-Study and other student funding
  • Change, extend, or end a student assignment between terms
